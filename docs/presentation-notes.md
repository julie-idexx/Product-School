# Speaker Notes — Streakly Comeback Screen Quarterly Review

*Full sentences, written to be said out loud. Calibrated to Marcus per `04-team/stakeholders/marcus.md`: recommendation up front, tied to the Day-7 number he owns, honest about what isn't proven yet.*

## Slide 1 — The Problem

Before I walk through this, here's where we're headed: I have one specific ask today, with a timeline attached, and everything on the next five slides builds toward it. Let's start with the number you already track — Day-7 retention dropped from 48% to 39% after the v2 streak and notification redesign. That's not spread evenly across our user base; it's concentrated in one specific moment: once someone misses two days in a row, about 80% of them never come back. That's the insight that matters — this isn't a slow leak, it's a single point of failure we can actually target.

## Slide 2 — Why Now

You already approved the problem framing last time we talked — this slide is what's changed since then, not a re-litigation of why the drop happened. Three independent research methods — user interviews, NPS feedback, and a competitive study — all converged on the same fix: nobody in this category offers a real way back in after a streak breaks, only ways to prevent it before. We didn't stop at that finding. We built the thing, put it in front of real users, and ran it as a live randomized test, so what I'm about to show you is data, not a pitch.

## Slide 3 — The Proposal

Here's exactly what we're proposing, and just as importantly, what we're not. The Comeback screen shows a returning user their best-ever streak and gives them a 60-second lesson to restart that same day — that's the full v1 scope, nothing more. What it isn't: it doesn't retroactively restore the streak they already lost — no competitor in the category does that, and we're not trying to be the first. And it's not a request to flip this on for everyone today; what I'm asking for later in this deck is permission to keep validating it at a larger scale before we do that.

## Slide 4 — Evidence

This is the evidence, and I want to be straight with you about what it does and doesn't prove yet. We piloted this on a real randomized split — half of one signup cohort saw the Comeback screen, half didn't — and the users who saw it retained at 76% on Day-7 versus 46% for the control group, a 30-point lift that's statistically significant. The engagement data backs it up too: people opened the follow-up messages more each time, 28% up to 56%, not less — the opposite of the notification fatigue we worried about. The one honest gap is Day-30: the lift there is still positive but not yet statistically proven, and I don't want to oversell that part.

## Slide 5 — The Plan

So here's the plan, and it's deliberately not "flip it on for everyone." We move the current version into the Q3 sprint as-is, and in parallel we run a two-week extension at a larger sample size specifically on the users who broke their streak, since that's the exact group the Day-30 number needs to prove out on. At the end of those two weeks, we make a real go or no-go call on full rollout — not an open-ended "let's keep watching it." The risk of not doing this is straightforward: every week we wait, the roughly 80% of week-one streak-breakers who currently never come back keep leaving at that same rate, on a problem we've already shown a working fix for.

## Slide 6 — The Ask

So here's the actual ask, and it's two things. First, approval to move this into the Q3 sprint at the scope I just described. Second, approval to run that two-week validation extension, with a hard go/no-go date at the end of it, so this doesn't turn into an indefinite "let's keep an eye on it." I'm not asking you to bet on Day-30 today — I'm asking for two more weeks to get you a number you can actually take to the board.
