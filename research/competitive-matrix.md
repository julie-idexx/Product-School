# Competitive Matrix — Streak & Habit Apps

**Scope:** Apps built around streaks, daily habits, or micro-learning. Excludes social-media and long-form course platforms, per brief. Researched September 2026 — pricing and feature details change often; treat as directional and re-verify before quoting externally.

**Competitors selected:** Duolingo, Babbel, Elevate (as specified), plus Brilliant.org (STEM/coding micro-learning) and Streaks (Crunchy Bagel's iOS habit tracker) — added for range across gamified language learning, structured language learning, brain training, STEM micro-learning, and a pure habit tracker with no lesson content at all.

---

## Duolingo

- **Core features:** Gamified bite-sized lessons (language, plus chess/math/music). XP, Hearts (lives), Gems (currency), weekly Leagues, streaks, and an AI Video Call conversation feature on the top paid tier.
- **Pricing:** Free tier (ads, limited hearts). Super Duolingo ≈ $12.99/mo or ≈ $95.99/yr (ad-free, unlimited hearts). Max tier ≈ $14/mo equivalent (AI features) — reports suggest Max may be getting scaled back, unconfirmed. Family plan ≈ $119.99/yr for 6 accounts.
- **Target customer:** ~130M MAU, ~46.6M DAU; skews 16–34 (~60% of DAU); broad income range via freemium; top markets US/Brazil/Mexico.
- **Post-week-1 engagement / comeback mechanics:** Streak freeze (purchased with gems, applied in advance of a miss). Streak repair (Super subscribers get one free repair per month for an already-broken streak). **Streak Revival** (new in 2026): a limited-time campaign letting users resurrect a long-dead streak by completing 3 lessons in one sitting. Push notifications (1B+/year) escalate in emotional intensity the longer a user has been inactive.
- **Notable recent changes:** April 2025 "AI-first" internal memo triggered public backlash (streak-deletion protests); 2026 declared an "investment year" prioritizing free-tier AI and DAU growth (target 100M DAU) over near-term monetization; Chess expanded to Android; LinkedIn integration surfacing a "Duolingo Score."

## Babbel

- **Core features:** Structured 10–15 min lessons (vocabulary, grammar, listening, speaking) with explicit grammar teaching — deliberately less gamified than Duolingo ("structure over streaks and leaderboards"). Babbel Speak (AI voice conversation partner, beta since Sept 2025), speech recognition, short podcasts, mistake-review sessions.
- **Pricing:** No permanent free tier, but first lesson of every course is free. Paid: ≈ $14.99/mo month-to-month, ≈ $8.97/mo on a 12-month plan (≈ $107.64/yr), lifetime ≈ $299. One subscription covers all 14 languages. Babbel Live (live classes) $99–$249/mo. New: first shared/family subscription (up to 6 users).
- **Target customer:** Self-motivated adult learners wanting a structured curriculum in one language — more serious/exam/travel/career-driven than Duolingo's casual-gamified base.
- **Post-week-1 engagement / comeback mechanics:** 2 automatic streak freezes per week (applied without user action if a day is missed). Device-level push reminders at a user-chosen time. **No distinct comeback/win-back flow found** — a plausible gap even within Babbel's own product.
- **Notable recent changes:** Babbel Speak AI voice beta; expanded AI Conversation Partner across 4 languages; first shared subscription tier; named to the 2026 GSV 150 list.

## Elevate (Elevate Labs)

- **Core features:** 40+ adaptive brain-training games (communication, math, memory, focus), personalized via an initial assessment and tracked with a proprietary 0–5000 "Elevate Proficiency Quotient." Daily Workout plus puzzle content (Daily Crossword, "Colorlink").
- **Pricing:** Free tier limited to ~3 games/day, no skill selection. Pro ≈ $9.99/mo or ≈ $39.99/yr.
- **Target customer:** General adults seeking cognitive/productivity improvement — positioned as "mental fitness," not tied to a specific skill or exam.
- **Post-week-1 engagement / comeback mechanics:** Every user gets 2 streak freezes automatically at signup; freezes auto-apply on a missed day; more are earned at streak milestones (3, 7, 14, 30... up to 365+ days). Streak only resets to 0 once freezes run out, and restarts at 1 the next completed day with no shame-driven messaging — a notably softer framing than a hard reset. Configurable push reminder time. Leagues (added 2025) add a social/competitive layer.
- **Notable recent changes:** Addition of Leagues and daily puzzle content (2025) — expanding beyond the core cognitive workout, no major pricing or streak-mechanic change found.

## Brilliant.org

- **Core features:** Interactive "learn by doing" STEM courses (90+, math/CS/data science/science) — hands-on problem-solving instead of video lectures. **Koji**, a real-time AI tutor launched 2026, watches the learner's actual problem and gives Socratic guidance rather than answers.
- **Pricing:** Freemium (limited intro content free). Premium ≈ $13.49/mo billed annually (≈ $161.88/yr) or ≈ $27.99/mo month-to-month. Family plan for up to 6.
- **Target customer:** "Ambitious learners ages 10–110" — STEM students needing fundamentals help, professionals refreshing skills, curious lifelong learners. Skews toward people already motivated by the subject matter rather than general habit-builders.
- **Post-week-1 engagement / comeback mechanics:** **Streak Charges** — earned automatically per completed lesson (max 2 banked); a charge is consumed instead of resetting the streak on a missed day. This is a proactive grace buffer, not a post-lapse recovery flow. **No evidence found of a win-back campaign, lapsed-user notification sequence, or "repair after reset" mechanic** — flagged as a likely gap for Brilliant specifically.
- **Notable recent changes:** Koji AI tutor launch (2026) is the major development; no pricing, redesign, or notification-system changes found in the past 12 months.

## Streaks (Crunchy Bagel)

- **Core features:** To-do-list-style habit tracker (up to 24 daily tasks) — no lesson content at all, just tracking. Check-off, timed, or "negative" (habit-to-break) tasks. HealthKit auto-completion, iCloud sync across Apple devices, widgets, Siri Shortcuts, 78 themes.
- **Pricing:** One-time purchase, no subscription — ≈ $5.99 on the App Store. No free tier.
- **Target customer:** Apple-only power users who want a flexible, quiet tool rather than a gamified/social app. 2016 Apple Design Award winner; 4.8★ across 27K ratings.
- **Post-week-1 engagement / comeback mechanics:** **"2-Day Rule"** (added 2024) — one missed day is shown as "skipped" without resetting the streak; a second consecutive miss resets it. Per-task automatic reminders. Task sharing with an accountability partner as the main social hook. Tasks can be explicitly "paused" for planned breaks, rather than broken.
- **Notable recent changes:** Streaks 11 (Sept 2025) redesign, medication tracking, timed tasks with system alarms; continuous minor updates through mid-2026.

## White Space for Streakly

**1. A real post-break recovery experience, not just pre-break prevention.** Every competitor's primary answer to "you might miss a day" is a grace buffer earned *before* the lapse happens (a freeze, a charge, a skip allowance). Once a user actually breaks through that buffer and the streak is gone, only Duolingo offers anything at all — a 2026 marketing campaign, one-time and generic, not a personalized flow. Nobody offers a moment that says "here's what you built, here's an easy way back in" once the streak has already hit zero. This is precisely the gap Streakly's own users named directly ("no way to recover it," "make coming back easier instead of... start from scratch") and precisely what the Comeback screen concept (best-streak stat + comeback lesson + freeze) is positioned to own.

**2. A home screen and messaging that adapts to where the user actually is.** None of the five apps researched show evidence of a UI that visibly changes its tone or content based on lapse status — Duolingo varies notification *copy* by inactivity length, but the in-app experience itself isn't shown to adapt. This matches Streakly's own NPS finding almost exactly: "the home screen looks the same whether I'm on a 2-day streak or coming back after two weeks away." An interface that visibly acknowledges a 4-day-in new user's fragility differently than a 14-month veteran's lapse would be a distinct, unclaimed position — not just a feature nobody has, but a design principle nobody in this set has adopted.
