# Interview Synthesis — Streak & Comeback Experience

**Sources:** Priya S. (power user, 14-month streak), Tom R. (churned, left after breaking a 12-day streak at 5 weeks in), Amara L. (new user, 4 days in).

## Top 5 Themes

### 1. The streak is the emotional core of the product — it drives both attachment and dread

The same mechanic that makes long-term users feel proud and ritualized makes new users anxious before they've even lost anything.

- Priya: *"I open Streakly every morning with my coffee. It has become a ritual."*
- Tom: *"I got to a 12-day streak and I was proud of it."*

### 2. The all-or-nothing reset feels like punishment, not motivation

Breaking a streak doesn't just cost progress — it's experienced as losing everything at once, with nothing offered in return.

- Tom: *"Then I traveled for work, missed two days, and came back to a big fat zero... There was no way to recover it, nothing."*
- Amara: *"I keep thinking, what happens if I miss a day? I don't want to lose everything I've built after four days."*

### 3. The post-break notification makes a bad moment worse

The one direct account of the break experience describes the notification itself as adding insult to injury, not softening the blow.

- Tom: *"The app sent me this 'you lost your streak' notification that just made me feel bad."*
- Tom: *"There was no way to recover it, nothing. So I gave up."*

### 4. Habit formation takes weeks, and users are most fragile before it sets in

The user furthest along names the exact risk window the other two are living through.

- Priya: *"It took me about three weeks to get there, and I think most people quit long before the habit forms."*
- Amara: *"The first few lessons were genuinely fun... but the pressure is starting to feel like a chore instead of a game."* (4 days in)

### 5. Forgiving mechanics (like a streak freeze) are now a competitive expectation

Only one user directly compared Streakly to an alternative, but the comparison is pointed and specific — noting it rather than forcing a second quote that isn't there.

- Tom: *"I switched to Duolingo, at least there a missed day doesn't wipe everything, and a streak freeze feels forgiving."*

## Contradictions / Tensions

- **Different theories of why people quit.** Priya attributes churn to habit not having formed yet ("most people quit long before the habit forms" — a *time* problem). Tom's actual churn wasn't about an unformed habit — he had a real 12-day streak and was proud of it; he left because of how the product responded to a lapse (a *mechanic/tone* problem). These point to different root causes and potentially different fixes.
- **The same streak mechanic produces opposite emotions depending on tenure.** For Priya, the streak (and its celebration at 30 days) is what made the habit "stick." For Amara, only 4 days in, that same streak is already a source of stress rather than motivation — "it's starting to feel like a chore instead of a game." What retains a 14-month user may be actively pressuring a first-week user.

## Single Most Important Insight for Week-1 Retention

Amara — the only user still in her first week — is already anxious about losing her streak on day 4, before she has broken anything. That means the fear of the punishing reset is a drag on early engagement well before any actual lapse occurs. Combined with Priya's observation that habit formation takes about three weeks and "most people quit long before" that point, this suggests the week-1 retention problem isn't only about how Streakly responds *after* a streak breaks (the Comeback screen concept already on the table) — it's also about the anticipatory dread during the days *before* a lapse ever happens. A PM optimizing for week-1 retention should treat the fear of breaking a streak, not just the aftermath of breaking one, as something to design against — for example, signaling early that a missed day won't erase everything, rather than letting new users spend their first week bracing for it.
